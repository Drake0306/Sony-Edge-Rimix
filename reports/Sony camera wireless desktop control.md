# Desktop Wireless Sony Control Is Buildable Today

**Bottom line:** a macOS-first, Windows-second desktop app that controls Sony Alpha bodies over Wi-Fi the way Creators' App does is feasible right now, by one of two proven routes, and the thing that makes the phone feel "wireless" while the desktop feels "USB-only" is not the control protocol but the connection bootstrap. Every 2020-and-later Alpha, ZV and FX body already runs a PTP/IP server on TCP 15740 for "Remote Shoot Function", reachable over Wi-Fi Direct or a shared router, with Sony's "Access Authentication" being nothing more exotic than an SSH tunnel whose username, password and host-key fingerprint are printed on the camera's screen ([Sony a7R V Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2230/v1/en/contents/TP0002927440.html); [jakkuh/sony-alpha-python connection.py](https://github.com/jakkuh/sony-alpha-python/blob/main/sony_camera/connection.py)). Sony's own free C++ **Camera Remote SDK 2.02.00** (June 2026) has supported Wi-Fi since v1.12 (April 2024), covers **31 bodies**, ships for Windows 11 x64 and macOS 14/15/26 as a universal library, and permits commercial apps that embed its binaries inseparably, though it forbids redistributing the SDK itself ([Sony SDK portal](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/sdk/en/index.html); [SDK licence](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/sdk/licenseagreement/en-US.html)). Independently, at least five open-source projects have reverse-engineered the same handshake without the SDK and validated it on the a6700, a7R V, FX30, FX3 and ZV-E10 ([Knallli/alpha2go](https://github.com/Knallli/alpha2go); [sarnau/sony-camera-protocol](https://github.com/sarnau/sony-camera-protocol)). What the desktop cannot fully replicate is Creators' App's Bluetooth bootstrap, powering the camera on and handing it Wi-Fi credentials over BLE, because Sony's BLE pairing exchange is undocumented; but one desktop Python script already pulls the camera's SSID and password over BLE on an a7R V, and a webcam QR scan plus programmatic Wi-Fi join plus SSDP discovery gets you most of the way with documented APIs. The hard boundary is camera generation: a7 III / a6400-class bodies (2017-2019) have no Wi-Fi PC Remote at all and are outside the SDK, so an app for them is either USB or the dying legacy JSON API. The closest fork candidate is **RemoteCam** (MIT, Win+Mac, CrSDK over Wi-Fi, released November 2026); the best protocol study is **alpha2go** (Apache-2.0, pure Go, SSH + PTP/IP, the most complete documented op coverage); the recommended architecture is a C++ CrSDK sidecar process behind a Tauri v2 (or native SwiftUI + WinUI) shell, with an SDK-free PTP/IP path held in reserve.

## Sony splits its lineup: BLE-native phone apps, USB-first desktop apps

Sony's current umbrella is **Creators' Cloud**, and its apps divide cleanly by radio. Every mobile app (Creators' App, Monitor & Control, Transfer & Tagging) is built around Bluetooth LE as the control plane that discovers, wakes and auto-joins the camera to Wi-Fi; no Sony desktop app uses Bluetooth at all, and no BLE step appears anywhere in the Imaging Edge Desktop or Remote Camera Tool documentation ([Imaging Edge Desktop: Connecting the camera](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/imagingedge/en/instruction/4_1_connection.php)). That said, the "desktop is USB-only" folklore is only half true: **Imaging Edge Desktop Remote has supported Wi-Fi tethering since version 2.0.00 on 2019-08-07**, in both Wi-Fi Direct (laptop joins the camera's `DIRECT-xxxx` SSID) and Wi-Fi Access Point mode (shared router, with first-time Pairing on older bodies or username/password/fingerprint "Access Authentication" on newer ones), plus wired LAN on the a1/a1 II/a9 II/a9 III/ILX-LR1 ([Imaging Edge Desktop support page](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/imagingedge/en/index.php); [Connecting the camera](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/imagingedge/en/instruction/4_1_connection.php)). The catch is that Sony's own compatibility grouping leaves most of the installed base out: "ZV-1F/1M2 and ZV-1A/1: USB only", "Other cameras: USB only, using the PC Remote setting in the MENU" ([Connecting the camera](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/imagingedge/en/instruction/4_1_connection.php)), and the a7 III help guide describes Remote only "from a computer connected to the camera with a USB cable" ([Sony a7 III Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/1720/v1/en/contents/TP0001667702.html)).

Two details of Sony's design shape everything downstream. First, the camera is **mutually exclusive between phone and PC**: "When the camera and a smartphone are connected, you cannot control the camera from a computer" ([Sony a7R V Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2230/v1/en/contents/TP0002927440.html)), so a desktop app competes with, rather than coexists with, Creators' App. Second, the desktop Wi-Fi path has a documented reliability problem that is mostly a discovery problem: Sony's FAQ blames firewalls, the macOS 15 "Local Network" permission, VPNs and other camera vendors' background items ([Imaging Edge Desktop FAQ](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/imagingedge/en/faqs/)), and users report Wi-Fi Direct working "pretty easily" on an a7 IV + MacBook while AP mode left the Mac unable to see a camera that was plainly on the router ([Alpha Shooters thread](https://alphashooters.com/community/threads/sony-a7-iv-wireless-tethering-with-imaging-edge-on-macbook-pro.4924)). A January 2026 forum thread is titled "It's 2026 and wireless tether options still suck" ([Fred Miranda topic 1929812](https://www.fredmiranda.com/forum/topic/1929812/)). That is the opening for a third-party app: not a new protocol, but a better bootstrap and better resilience.

## What mobile can do that desktop cannot (and the reverse)

The gap is narrower than it looks and almost entirely about **getting connected**, not about what you can do once connected. Over Wi-Fi the PTP control surface available to a desktop is actually broader for stills than what Creators' App exposes: full-resolution RAW to disk, interval timer, and every device property, versus the phone's default of 2M-pixel copies and no video transfer ([Creators' App: Cameras screen](https://helpguide.sony.net/di-app/ca/v1/en/contents/CA_camera_screen.html); [Imaging Edge Desktop: Remote Shooting](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/imagingedge/en/instruction/4_5_remote.php)). What the phone has and the desktop lacks is the BLE control plane and the cloud/firmware functions Sony chose to route only through the phone.

## Under the hood: SSDP, PTP/IP on 15740, SSH, and a thin BLE layer

Sony publishes no port numbers for its consumer apps, but three independent implementations plus Wireshark-derived notes converge on the same wire sequence, and Sony's SDK documentation inadvertently confirms it by listing the ports its own library opens: "Remote port UDP 1900, 32768-61000; TCP 80, 8080, 22, 64321, 15740" ([API ref: Environment Setup](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/other/environment_setup.html)). UDP 1900 is SSDP, TCP 64321 serves the UPnP device description, TCP 22 is the SSH tunnel and TCP 15740 is ISO PTP/IP.

**Discovery.** The camera advertises via SSDP on 239.255.255.250:1900 and serves `http://<ip>:64321/dd.xml`, which links `DigitalImagingDesc.xml` containing `<X_PTP_Information>` with `X_PTP_Versions` (e.g. `3.00`), `X_PTP_PairingNecessity`, `X_PTP_RemoteControlSupport` and `<X_SSH_Support>` ([rocc-discovery xml.rs](https://github.com/Streetblock/remote-camera-control-rs/blob/main/crates/rocc-discovery/src/xml.rs)). In Wi-Fi Direct the camera is almost always **192.168.122.1** (older bodies 10.0.0.1 or 192.168.0.1) ([schorschii/ImagingEdge4Linux](https://github.com/schorschii/ImagingEdge4Linux)). No source found evidence of mDNS/Bonjour advertisement; discovery is UPnP/SSDP, ARP, or a port-15740 probe (alpha2go implements all three) ([alpha2go](https://github.com/Knallli/alpha2go)). The SDK's `EnumCameraObjects()` finds cameras only on the same L2 segment and notes "about 30 seconds" for a wired camera to appear after IP setup; across subnets you build a camera object manually from IP and an arbitrary 6-byte identifier ("It is not always necessary to specify the MAC address of the camera body") ([API ref: Camera Object](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/api_reference/camera_object.html)).

**Authentication.** With Access Authen. On, the client opens SSH to the camera and forwards a local port to `localhost:15740`; sony-alpha-python does it with literally `ssh -N -L <local>:localhost:15740 -o PreferredAuthentications=keyboard-interactive <user>@<camera>` and reports SSH "far more reliable" than the pairing path ([sony-alpha-python](https://github.com/jakkuh/sony-alpha-python)). The fingerprint the camera displays is the SSH host key; the SDK's `GetFingerprint()` doc says comparing it "is the only way to avoid connecting to the wrong destination", and it is Base64, cacheable, and changes only on camera initialisation ([API ref: Camera Object](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/api_reference/camera_object.html)). Two hazards: "Repeatedly entering the wrong SSH parameters will lock the camera" (power-cycle to clear) ([API ref: Connection](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/api_reference/connection.html)), and the non-SSH **GUID pairing** path cannot be undone short of a factory reset, which is why alpha-fairy's author recommends "Connect Without Pairing" ([alpha-fairy RE notes](https://github.com/frank26080115/alpha-fairy/blob/main/doc/Camera-Reverse-Engineering.md)). No open-source project has tested the pairing path on a Gen 4 body with Access Authen. turned off ([alpha2go camera-setup](https://github.com/Knallli/alpha2go/blob/main/docs/camera-setup.md)).

**Control.** After the standard PTP/IP `Init_Command_Request` (16-byte GUID + UTF-16 host name) and `OpenSession`, Sony's "SDIO" vendor extension takes over: `SDIO_Connect 0x9201` with parameters 1, 2 and 3, `SDIO_GetExtDeviceInfo 0x9202`, then `SDIO_GetAllExtDevicePropInfo 0x9209` for the full property blob, `SDIO_SetExtDevicePropValue 0x9205` to write, `SDIO_ControlDevice 0x9207` for shutter/AF, and live view via `GetObject(0xFFFFC002)` ([libgphoto2 ptp.h](https://github.com/gphoto/libgphoto2/blob/master/camlibs/ptp2/ptp.h); [sony-alpha-python protocol.py](https://github.com/jakkuh/sony-alpha-python/blob/main/sony_camera/protocol.py)). Events (property changed, object added) arrive on a second TCP connection. Measured on an a7R V over Wi-Fi: live view at **25.7 fps**, a 60 MP download at ~12 MB/s, RAW off the card at ~16 MB/s, 446 properties decoded of which 190 are writable ([sarnau](https://github.com/sarnau/sony-camera-protocol)); alpha2go streams 1024x680 at ~30 fps with level/focus/face frames and the OSD as a PNG ([alpha2go](https://github.com/Knallli/alpha2go)). The protocol is now officially documented by Sony: libgphoto2 2.5.31's notes say "Sony: Now officially documented by Sony" and the Camera Remote Command reference exists, but only for corporate customers ([libgphoto2 NEWS](https://github.com/gphoto/libgphoto2/blob/master/NEWS); [Camera Remote Command portal](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/cameraremotecommand/en/index.html)).

**BLE.** Cameras advertise manufacturer data with Sony company ID 0x012D and status bits (PairingEnabled, WifiHandoverSupported, WirelessPowerOnEnabled), and expose four services: `8000CC00` Camera Control (Wi-Fi handoff: SSID in CC06, password in CC07), `8000DD00` Location, `8000EE00` Pairing and `8000FF00` Remote Control with 16-bit button codes written to FF01 ([Hypoxic BLE write-up](https://gethypoxic.com/blogs/technical/sony-camera-ble-control-protocol-di-remote-control); [coral/freemote](https://github.com/coral/freemote)). The remote and GPS services are fully reverse-engineered and shipped; the Wi-Fi handoff is proven on a desktop by sarnau; the EE00 pairing exchange that produces Creators' App's six-digit code is not public. The Android α-Remote author is blunt that ISO/shutter/aperture are "not possible via Bluetooth" ([Staacks/alpharemote](https://github.com/Staacks/alpharemote)); BLE's value is onboarding, wake detection, shutter and geotag, never control.

**QR code.** On Imaging Edge Mobile-era bodies the QR encodes the camera's `DIRECT-xxxx` SSID and password ([Sony a1 Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2040/v1/en/contents/TP1000416941.html)); on Creators' App-era bodies the on-screen QR is just an app-store link and the connection is BLE ([Sony a7 V Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2540/v1/en/contents/221h_creators_app.html)). No public source documents the literal payload grammar; this must be settled by scanning a real camera with a generic reader. Gen 4 bodies still show SSID/password under "Wi-Fi Direct Info", so a QR-less Wi-Fi Direct flow is always available.

**Legacy JSON API.** The 2013-2017 Camera Remote API (JSON-RPC at `http://<cam>:10000/sony/<service>`) was archived by Sony in 2020 in favour of the SDK, with the explicit note that its cameras "are not included in compatible cameras' line-up for Camera Remote SDK" ([Sony press release 2020-02-12](https://sony.mediaroom.com/2020-02-12-Sony-Electronics-Announces-New-Camera-Software-Development-Kit-for-Third-party-Developers-and-Integrators)). It is irrelevant for any 2018+ body.

## The official SDK: free, C++, Wi-Fi since 2024, awkward to package

The Camera Remote SDK is "a suite of C++ classes and binary libraries": headers plus `libCr_Core`, `libmonitor_protocol`, and a `CrAdapter/` plugin folder containing `Cr_PTP_IP`, `Cr_PTP_USB`, `libssh2` and `libusb-1.0` ([kchinzei/CrSDKHelper](https://github.com/kchinzei/CrSDKHelper); [API ref: Environment Setup](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/other/environment_setup.html)). Those adapter names settle the protocol question: the SDK's network transport is PTP/IP with libssh2 for Access Authentication, the same stack the open-source projects rebuilt. Access is by region through pro.sony download or contact forms, and the licence (Sony Electronics Inc., US law, AAA arbitration) grants the right to "incorporate the SDK's binary library into your app in an inseparable way" and sell it, while forbidding separate distribution of the SDK, requiring you to tell end users that controlling the camera via your app voids Sony's warranty, and banning military ISR uses ([SDK licence](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/sdk/licenseagreement/en-US.html)). Every open-source wrapper therefore ships without the SDK and makes users download it; a commercial app that bundles the dylibs inside its signed `.app` is squarely permitted. One commercial developer describes registration as "free" and keeps the SDK in a private vendor repo for CI ([eclipseClick blog 2026-03-26](https://eclipseclick-website.pages.dev/blog/supporting-sony-cameras-eclipseclick)); Camera Remote Command, by contrast, is "only available for corporate customers" ([Camera Remote Command portal](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/cameraremotecommand/en/index.html)).

The connection API is small and maps directly onto the desired UX: `EnumCameraObjects()` to auto-discover; `CreateCameraObjectInfoEthernetConnection(model, ipv4, mac[6], sshSupport)` for a user-entered IP; `GetFingerprint()` to fetch the Base64 fingerprint for the confirmation screen; `Connect(..., userId, userPassword, fingerprint, size, pairingDisplayName)` with `openMode` Remote / ContentsTransfer / RemoteTransfer and auto-reconnect on by default ([API ref: Connection](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/api_reference/connection.html)). The Linux tool sonshell shows the robust pattern: enumerate, prefer the discovered fingerprint, fall back to a cached one, and if `Connect` fails with 0x8202 retry once with the opposite `CrSSHsupport` flag because bodies can have Access Authen. toggled off ([goudvuur/sonshell src/main.cpp](https://github.com/goudvuur/sonshell/blob/master/src/main.cpp)). The SDK then exposes hundreds of `CrDeviceProperty_*` with enable flags and candidate lists, control codes for every button and dial, polled JPEG live view, three content-transfer paths, FTP job control, settings files, remote firmware update, PTZ and multi-camera connect ([API ref index](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/index.html)).

The pain is operational, and it is well documented by people who shipped. On macOS, `libCr_Core` **hard-codes plugin lookup to `Contents/Frameworks/CrAdapter`** with no fallback, the dylibs ship as separate arm64 and x86_64 slices, `ptpcamerad` races the SDK for USB sessions, and calling `Init()` after `Release()` in the same process segfaults, which is why eclipseClick exits and respawns its bridge process after every disconnect ([eclipseClick blog 2026-04-28](https://eclipseclick-website.pages.dev/blog/sony-crsdk-macos-apple-silicon)). Sony requires stripping quarantine attributes from all seven dylibs after download, and nothing indicates they are notarized; a shipping app must re-sign them under its own Developer ID with hardened runtime ([API ref: Environment Setup](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/other/environment_setup.html)). On Windows, USB requires Sony's libusbK driver that replaces the MTP driver, a frequent source of conflicts with Lightroom and Capture One ([Kiriusss/Sony-Camera-Control](https://github.com/Kiriusss/Sony-Camera-Control); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-bugs/p-cannot-tether-sony-a7-iv-ilce-7m4-with-latest-3-01-firmware/idi-p/14630158)), a problem a Wi-Fi-first app sidesteps entirely. The alpha-sdk-api issue tracker catalogues the runtime failure modes to design around: `ReleaseDevice()` hanging forever after a stuck transfer, RemoteTransfer downloads that "silently never complete", and `0x8208` timeouts misattributed to camera settings ([alpha-sdk-api#41](https://github.com/crsdk/alpha-sdk-api/issues/41); [#40](https://github.com/crsdk/alpha-sdk-api/issues/40); [#39](https://github.com/crsdk/alpha-sdk-api/issues/39)). A Bitfocus contributor summarised the community mood: "the SDK is incredibly hard to work with and each camera is different" ([bitfocus/companion-module-requests#1936](https://github.com/bitfocus/companion-module-requests/issues/1936)). None of this is disqualifying; all of it argues for isolating the SDK in its own process.

## Implementation options: isolate the camera layer, then pick a shell

Two decisions are independent: which **camera-layer route** to take, and which **UI shell** to wrap it in. The research is unambiguous on one architectural point: both real-world CrSDK integrations found in the wild (eclipseClick's C++ bridge over JSON-RPC on stdio, and the `camera-remote-web-api` native binary with REST + WebSocket live view) chose to run the SDK **out of process**, because of the segfault-on-reinit, the hard-coded plugin path and the hangs ([eclipseClick blog](https://eclipseclick-website.pages.dev/blog/sony-crsdk-macos-apple-silicon); [camera-remote-web-api on npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/camera-remote-web-api)). A sidecar also means one C++ codebase for the camera layer regardless of UI technology.

Capture One's Sony wireless guide, which has users type the camera's Access Authen. credentials and disable them on some firmware, is consistent with either route ([Capture One Sony wireless tethering guide](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/articles/5473534946461-Sony-wireless-tethering-setup-guide)).

### UI shells

The hard parts are identical across frameworks: marshalling SDK callbacks from SDK threads to the UI thread, moving 15-30 JPEG frames per second without JSON-encoding them, and bundling Sony's binaries into a signed, notarized package ([desktop_implementation notes synthesising Electron, Tauri, Flutter, .NET docs](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/calling-rust/)). A 30 fps stream of 100-300 KB JPEGs is 3-9 MB/s, well within any IPC path; the risk is per-frame allocation on the UI thread.

Three OS-level realities apply to every shell. **Wi-Fi join** is technically possible on both OSes but gated behind Location permission (macOS 14.4+, Windows since late 2024), and macOS Sequoia showed SSID-redaction regressions that broke `associate(withName:)` in non-GUI processes, so Wi-Fi join must live in the GUI process and ship with a manual "join `DIRECT-xxxx`" fallback ([Apple forums 769950](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/769950); [MS Learn Wi-Fi location changes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/nativewifi/wi-fi-access-location-changes)). **BLE** on macOS never exposes the peripheral MAC and cannot unpair programmatically ([SimpleBLE platform notes](https://docs.simpleble.org/simpleble/platform_notes)). **Mac App Store sandboxing** conflicts with CoreWLAN network changes, so plan on direct Developer ID distribution.

Read across the five tables, the decision collapses to a handful of verdicts. **RemoteCam** is the only complete, cross-platform, MIT-licensed, actively released desktop app that does precisely what the user wants (Wi-Fi + Access Authentication + live view + capture on Win and Mac); its weaknesses are a single tested body, ~8 fps live view versus 25-30 fps in the direct-PTP/IP projects, a .NET + C++ stack the user may or may not want, and heavy AI-assisted authorship ([RemoteCam README](https://github.com/RemingtonWilcox/RemoteCam)). **alpha2go** is the most complete SDK-free implementation and the best protocol documentation in the open, but it is Go and one body ([alpha2go](https://github.com/Knallli/alpha2go)). **sarnau/sony-camera-protocol** is the single most valuable study for the onboarding problem because it is the only desktop code that replaces both QR and IP entry with a BLE handshake ([sarnau](https://github.com/sarnau/sony-camera-protocol)). **alphawire** is the only open-source desktop GUI and the only C library spanning pre- and post-2020 bodies, but lacks SSH. **sonshell** has the most defensively written SDK Wi-Fi connection code. The features multiple independent authors re-implemented because Sony's desktop app lacks them, and therefore the lowest-risk differentiators, are presets, auto-reconnect, scopes and LUT monitoring, a phone or browser second screen, and an automation API (REST, MCP, `--json`).

## Recommended architecture: CrSDK sidecar behind a thin shell, PTP/IP in reserve

The recommendation is a **three-layer design with the camera protocol in its own process**. Layer one is a C++ "camera bridge" sidecar that links the Camera Remote SDK, exposes a local control channel (JSON-RPC over stdio or a localhost WebSocket) and a binary live-view channel (WebSocket frames or MJPEG), owns reconnect and fingerprint caching, and is designed to be killed and respawned after every disconnect because of the `Init()`-after-`Release()` crash ([eclipseClick blog](https://eclipseclick-website.pages.dev/blog/sony-crsdk-macos-apple-silicon)). Layer two is the connection manager, in the **GUI process** because CoreWLAN and location permissions misbehave in helpers: webcam QR decode (`zxing-wasm` or Vision), programmatic Wi-Fi join with a manual fallback, SSDP M-SEARCH plus `DigitalImagingDesc.xml` parsing to learn `X_SSH_Support` and `X_PTP_PairingNecessity`, a default-gateway shortcut for Wi-Fi Direct (the camera is the gateway at 192.168.122.1), optional BLE scan for the `0x012D` advertisement to show "camera nearby, pairing enabled" and, later, the CC06/CC07 credential read, and a first-connect fingerprint confirmation screen whose result is cached per camera serial. Layer three is the UI: **Tauri v2** if the developer is web-stack-first (8-22 MB bundles, `Channel<&[u8]>` for frames, `btleplug` for BLE, `externalBin` for the sidecar), or **SwiftUI on macOS followed by WinUI 3 / .NET 10 on Windows** sharing the same C++ bridge if native integration (Vision, CoreWLAN, CoreBluetooth) matters more than a single UI codebase. Electron is the fastest prototype but costs 10-20x the download for no protocol advantage.

Phase the work by risk. Phase 1 is the bridge plus manual IP entry plus Access Authentication plus live view plus capture-to-disk over a shared router on one Gen 4 body, which is exactly the surface RemoteCam and sonshell already cover and the biggest debugging sink (SDK threading, `CrAdapter` layout, re-signing seven dylibs under hardened runtime, the Windows firewall exception). Phase 2 is onboarding: SSDP discovery, QR scan, Wi-Fi join, fingerprint UX, auto-reconnect, Wi-Fi Direct fallback. Phase 3 is the differentiators the community keeps rebuilding: presets, scopes and LUT preview on the JPEG stream, a browser second screen, interval and bracketing, a REST or `--json` automation surface, and an SFTP receiver so the camera can push files when control is not needed. Phase 4, optional, is the SDK-free PTP/IP path (port alpha2go's Go or wrap `remote-camera-control-rs`) for bodies the SDK excludes over Wi-Fi (a7R IV, a7C, ZV-E10) and for BLE credential handoff; and BLE wake, only if the power-on command can be sniffed.

The recommendation rests on **explicit assumptions** the user should confirm or overturn. First, the target bodies are Gen 4 (Access Authentication era, 2022 onward) plus the a1/a7S III/a7 IV; if the user's own camera is an a7 III, a6400 or a6600, nothing here delivers wireless control and the honest options are USB (alphawire, libgphoto2, ImageCaptureCore) or the BLE shutter. Second, the user is willing to register for and accept Sony's SDK EULA, to distribute a signed binary rather than a pure source tree, and to surface the warranty-void and "Arms" disclosures ([SDK licence](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/sdk/licenseagreement/en-US.html)); if the project must be fully open source with contributors building from source, the SDK-free route becomes primary. Third, the primary topology is camera and laptop on the same router with Access Authentication On, Wi-Fi Direct as fallback; the SDK's Wi-Fi Direct behaviour is documented ("only this camera will be detected") but no CrSDK-based repo demonstrates it, whereas SonyCamViewer demonstrates it SDK-free ([API ref: Environment Setup](https://github.com/amarburg/sony_remote_camera_sdk/blob/main/other/environment_setup.html)). Fourth, the developer will hold an Apple Developer ID (notarization is mandatory; free accounts cannot notarize) and a Windows signing path (EV certificate or Azure Artifact Signing) ([Electron code signing](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/code-signing)). Fifth, polled JPEG live view at 15-30 fps is acceptable; neither route offers an H.264 stream. Sixth, a solo or very small team, which is why the sidecar's single C++ codebase and a single UI shell are weighted so heavily.

**Open questions for the user to decide**, in rough order of consequence: (1) Which exact bodies and firmware versions must work on day one, and is an a7 III-class body among them? (2) Open source with user-supplied SDK, source-available with a signed binary, or closed commercial? This picks Route A or B. (3) Web-stack (Tauri/Electron) or native (Swift + WinUI) comfort? (4) Is Wi-Fi Direct (laptop leaves the internet) or shared-router AP mode the primary UX? (5) Is BLE onboarding worth reverse-engineering the EE00 pairing exchange, or is QR-plus-SSDP enough? (6) Mac App Store (sandbox blocks CoreWLAN network changes) or direct DMG? (7) Are multi-camera linking and video scopes (Monitor & Control territory) in scope, or stills tethering first? (8) Should captures land in a hot folder for Lightroom or Capture One, which no surveyed repo does?

**Open questions the research could not close**, which should be settled by experiment before committing: the literal QR payload grammar (scan a real camera); whether Sony's dylibs load under hardened runtime without `disable-library-validation` entitlements and whether they are themselves signed (`codesign -dv`); whether CrSDK Wi-Fi works in Wi-Fi Direct topology on macOS and Windows; the per-interface support of the a7R VI in SDK 2.02.00; the camera's SSH server configuration beyond port 22 and the `aes128-ctr` cipher one author forces; the BLE power-on command; and the Gen 4 non-SSH pairing path, which no open project has exercised. Each is a one-afternoon test with a camera in hand, and each materially changes the onboarding design.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "Sony only lets the desktop use a USB cable" is a 2019 fact about the a7 III generation that has hardened into folklore; since 2019 every new Alpha has shipped a Wi-Fi PTP/IP remote server, since 2022 it has been protected by ordinary SSH with on-screen credentials, and since 2024 Sony's free SDK has spoken to it. What Sony has actually withheld from the desktop is the Bluetooth bootstrap and the cloud, not control. A third-party app's leverage therefore lies in the ten seconds between opening the laptop and seeing live view: QR or BLE instead of typed SSIDs, SSDP instead of "click Refresh", a cached fingerprint instead of a re-typed password, and reconnection that survives a sleep cycle. Those are solved problems in software, not protocol problems.

The second shift is in the ecosystem's centre of gravity. The 2013-2021 generation of Sony tooling (pysony, rocc, sony-pm-alt) is dead because its protocol is dead; the 2025-2026 wave (alpha2go, sarnau, RemoteCam, alphawire, SonyCamViewer) is tiny, single-body and often AI-assisted, but it is converging on one correct architecture and has, between it, already measured the throughput ceiling (25-30 fps live view, 12-16 MB/s RAW) that a new app can promise. The sensible move is not to pick one repo but to compose: RemoteCam's product shape and SDK bridge, sonshell's connection defensiveness, alpha2go's protocol documentation as the escape hatch when the SDK says no, and sarnau's BLE handshake as the eventual answer to "why can't my laptop do what my phone does."
